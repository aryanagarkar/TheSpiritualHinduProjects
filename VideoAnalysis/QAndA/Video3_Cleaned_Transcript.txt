**Question:** What's your recent discovery been?

**Answer (Curt Jaimungal):** It's not so much a discovery, it's more of a new way of thinking about it. One of the weird things about plenaria is that because they reproduce by tearing themselves in half and then each half grows the rest of the body, any mutation that doesn't kill the cell gets expanded into the Next Generation. What I think is happening is this: imagine if you had a passive material such that you've got some genes, the genes determine what the material does and so therefore you have an outcome and that outcome gets selected. By the time a nice tadpole goes up for selection to see whether it gets to reproduce or not, selection can't really see whether it looks good because the genome was great or because the genome was actually so-so but it fixed whatever issue it had. That competency starts to hide information from selection. So selection finds it kind of hard to choose the best genome because even the ones with problems look pretty good by the time it's time to be selected.
